Wednesday, November 18, 2026

Winter has arrived!

Update 11/19 PM:

Quick update: Remember that 20–30% I talked about the other day? Well, it looks like we’ve trended in the wrong direction towards those solutions, hence why we say it’s probabilistic. What happened? The bulk of Thursday PM / Friday AM's snow trended farther south; however, at least the Friday-night WNW/NW flow behind the front still looks solid and could produce decent totals. With that, I think it’s more likely we’ll end up in the lower half of the range now with the action primarily coming early Friday with a break midday before peaking late PM. Also, just wanted to add that snowfall for this storm will likely be lower density (not quite cold smoke but in that direction), which could help compensate for the storm track shifts a bit but isn't quite as conducive to base building. All that said, all snow is good snow :)

Original Post from 11/18 early AM:

Both the snow water equivalent (SWE) and the snowfall totals busted to the high side at Bridger! Right now Brackett Creek’s SNOTEL is around 9 inches and Bridger Bowl is at around 10–11 inches and still creeping up with a few more hours of post-frontal snow showers still to come. Ullr 🙌 The water equivalent is also quite impressive with anywhere from 0.6–1” of SWE. Big Sky looks like it’s hitting right within its upper range with around 5–7 inches being reported there. The water equivalent bust is great news, as that’s what is most important this time of year to build our base for the rest of the season.

Rest of the week: Things will clear out later today, and Thursday looks relatively dry and breezy with much colder temperatures than we’ve seen the past few weeks. Then a slow-moving low will track to our southwest, and we should get some of the wraparound moisture here in SW Montana. Right now, this next storm looks to begin Thursday PM and continue through Friday, with light snow showers possibly continuing into Saturday. Models are trending towards a wetter solution; however, the forecast numbers below hinge on that trend holding. I'd give things about a 20–30% chance of falling beneath those predicted ranges. I know often we want deterministic (yes, no, amount) type forecasts but it's very much probabilistic, so just figured I'd pass that along to you all as well.

ECMWF ensemble-average upper-level (500-mb) pattern, Wednesday through Monday morning.

Thursday through Saturday:

  • Bridger and Big Sky: 4–8 inches
  • Bozeman: 1–2 inches

Looking further ahead, a deep trough could set up off the West Coast early next week, so Thanksgiving week could be worth watching as well. Thanks for reading!

-Trey
