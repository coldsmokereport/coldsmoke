Welcome to The Cold Smoke Report

It's July, the Bridgers are green, and this is exactly the right time to start a snow blog — because the interesting work happens before the first flake.

The Cold Smoke Report is a new home for southwest Montana snow: daily forecast discussions for Bridger Bowl and Big Sky once the season spins up (with Bozeman in-town forecasts in the mix), plus a Season Tracker and a Climatology Explorer that run year-round.

The name is the local term of art. Cold smoke is the low-density powder the Bridgers are famous for — cold continental storms, high snow-to-liquid ratios, and the kind of snow that hangs in the air behind you.

A few numbers to set the table, from the 75-season station record behind this site:

  • Bridger Bowl's mean season delivers about 164 inches at the study stations, Big Sky about 141 — and the spread between a lean year and a fat one is enormous.
  • A top-10% snow day at Bridger is about 6 inches; the top-1% days push past a foot.
  • December through March do the heavy lifting, but the biggest single storms on record have a habit of showing up in the shoulder months.

Last winter (2025–26) finished at 139 inches at Bridger and 127 at Big Sky — you can see exactly how that season traced against climatology on the tracker.

When the snow returns, each morning's post will lead with a calibrated P10/P50/P90 forecast from a quantile ML system trained on that same record, followed by the human discussion of why. Until then: climatology season.
