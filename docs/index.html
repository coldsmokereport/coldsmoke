Monday, October 12, 2026

Snow Day!

What a beautiful morning looking up at the ridge! Overall, snowfall totals in the Bridgers hit on the upper end of forecasted ranges with 10–14" at mid-upper elevations and 1.5–2" of liquid water equivalent. Totals at Big Sky were a bit lower (4–7"), highlighting the very tight gradients of precipitation that made this forecast particularly tricky. The forecast in town was definitely a bust to the upside with 4–8" of snow. What happened? In my previous post you can see the stalled frontal boundary draped across the valley on Saturday. It pushed just far enough south to allow colder air to filter in about 18 hours earlier than was forecasted. Furthermore, the snow stuck better to the warm ground than expected. Hopefully you escaped any tree damage, since most of the in-town foliage has yet to fall.

If you want to try to get a couple of turns in, I think low-angle grassy slopes may work (tips up and rock skis only!) in the Bridgers with the high water content. You'll need to get after it today, though. Looking ahead, temperatures will be a bit below average for the next couple of days before we rapidly warm up into late week. Mid-60s by the weekend means most of the low-mid elevation snow won't last. At the upper elevations (especially north-facing) we're going to keep a wary eye out for faceting and the potential for a weak layer near the ground if we don't quickly either fully melt (hard to do this time of year but not impossible) or continue to get additional snowfall. Keep an eye on https://www.mtavalanche.com/ for updated backcountry forecasts. Thanks for reading!

-Trey

Saturday, October 10, 2026

Incoming

Hope you all were able to get outside the past few days with the great fall weather we've had! I was able to get in one last bike ride this afternoon (huge shoutout to SWMMBA, Crosscut, and Bridger for the awesome new trails up there!).

Hopefully this will all be covered in snow the next time we see it :)

Radar, temperatures and winds from this afternoon (the front's position is from 3 PM):

Currently a stalled frontal boundary is draped across SW Montana as two storm systems converge over the region: One from the west/northwest and the other, associated with Tropical Storm Rachel to the south. Precipitation should transition to snow at upper elevation sites overnight Saturday, and overall the forecast still looks to be on track, 6–12"+ above mid-mountain. The heaviest snow looks to fall Sunday afternoon through Monday morning. In Bozeman, expect 1–3"+ by Monday morning, with the heaviest snow Sunday PM into the overnight. There is still some uncertainty and a fairly strong gradient of moisture and precipitation, so there could be some big winners and losers. Do your snow dances!

Wednesday, October 7, 2026

Weekend Snow

Thanks, all, for the warm reception to the launch of this blog! It makes me excited that so many in our community are interested in the weather. Today’s post will cover this weekend’s potential storm. ❄️ ⛷🏂

It’s hard to believe with how warm it’s been over this past week (we’re just a few degrees shy of record highs for the date), but we may be talking about the potential for enough snow to ski early next week (at least for those with rock skis and flexible ACLs). Wait, what? Skiing already? Let me start off by saying that this is a very complex forecast. Why? We have a traditional frontal / mid-latitude storm system moving in from the west, but it’ll also be interacting with Hurricane Rachel that will be moving northward from the eastern Pacific. 

On Saturday PM a strong cold front will move through bringing heavy mountain precipitation that should quickly transition to snow at all elevations. Both the ECMWF (European weather model) and Google DeepMind (AI model) are trending more bullish with potential snowfall. Below is UofUtah’s downscaled snow ensemble (meaning it uses the large global GFS and ECMWF models and tries to “downscale” or extrapolate what precipitation might look like on smaller scales due to terrain effects, etc.). The GEFS and ECMWF ensembles in the Utah Snow Ensemble graphics below both show a mean of > 1" of water content. For early season storms we generally want more water content (less cold smoke) to help build our base. 

In the plots above you can see that both the GEFS and ECMWF ensembles are generally in decent agreement with the water content but less so on overall snowfall amounts. This is because the GEFS is quite a bit warmer than the ECMWF ensembles. Though I can’t completely discount that solution, it seems like it’s more of an outlier and I’d maybe lean towards the colder solutions with more snowfall based on current guidance.

With this, my current expectations would be 6–12”+ for the upper mountains at Bridger and Big Sky. Also, I think there's a decent likelihood of our first in-town snow in Bozeman overnight Saturday. Note that the IFS and GFS operational runs keep things a bit warmer and drier, which would make these totals lower. At this time, though, those models seem to be outliers. There's a pretty strong precipitation gradient from northwest - southeast that I'm keeping an eye on as well. I’ll also add that this is a quite complex forecast due to the interactions of multiple features here. Furthermore, I’d expect decreased predictability overall in the upcoming pattern for the next 2+ weeks. A former professor of mine writes a very interesting Utah-based blog (also helps run weather.utah.edu, where I got the plume graphic from) with a recent post on how western Pacific typhoons may be affecting the pattern downstream over the Intermountain West: https://wasatchweatherweenies.blogspot.com/2026/10/how-to-break-jet-stream.html.

And for the hardcore weather nerds: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2015GL065082

Thanks for reading!

-Trey

P.S. for our friends farther west near Missoula and the Bitterroots, totals look a bit lighter.
