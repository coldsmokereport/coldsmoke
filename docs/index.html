Saturday, November 7, 2026

Fall Never Leaves

Apologies for the silence and slow updates on my end. It’s been a busy week with the real job, and honestly, there also just hasn’t been a lot to talk about or update from a forecast perspective. We’ve been above average in terms of temperatures during this dry spell with a strong ridge overhead. This is sort of that hallmark El Niño-esque upper level ridge pattern we’d highlighted in our El Niño forecast post last month: https://coldsmokereport.com/posts/el-nino-and-bridger-bowl-record-winter/. Looking ahead, is there any hope for a pattern change on the horizon?

It looks like much of the same this upcoming week, though a weak storm may try to ride over the top of the upper-level ridge around mid-week. I wouldn’t expect too much beyond increased clouds and maybe a few showers. It won’t really move the needle any.

Daily high temperatures and forecast with respect to climatology.

The models are showing a dry cold front next weekend that will help knock temperatures down a bit. The earliest shot at real snow and seasonable cold looks like next Monday night into Tuesday (~10 days from now). The timing has been slipping in the models, which is never a great sign, and there's a real possibility the storm could stall off California. This one is pretty 50/50 at this point, and there's not really much confidence in it until we see a real signal within the 7 day range. If we’re looking for any hopium, the European ensemble does show a bigger storm in about 1-in-3 of its ensemble members.

In the meantime, if things stay quiet this upcoming week, I’ll try to do a more generic post on a topic like “Most favorable storm patterns for Bridger and Big Sky” or “Impacts of Climate Change on our Ski Areas”. I'm open to any other suggestions/ideas. Feel free to drop them in the comments! Thanks for reading!

Monday, November 2, 2026

Solid Snowfall Totals but a Concerning Forecast

I’m realizing that I’m facing a slight dilemma with forecast totals vs. reported totals. This goes back to the SNOTEL measurements and snowpack settling I discussed in my initial posts. The snowfall trackers on the right-hand side use 24h SNOTEL settled depth at 1 location for each, so they almost always are lower than what is reported at the resorts. However, I can’t track resort accumulations, at least for Big Sky, where that data is not publicly available. So when I make a forecast (e.g. 2-4 inches+), is that verified against the SNOTEL or resort wide?

For this storm we saw about 2–3” at most of the SNOTEL locations; however, the interval boards at Bridger reported 5 inches and Big Sky reported 6”, though it’s unclear where Big Sky is getting that 6 inches from (I assume they have an upper mountain snow stake, i.e., not on their webcams). If anybody knows feel free to comment :)

Outlook:

Unfortunately, it looks like we’re heading into a pattern with a ridge building over us that will lead to a dry spell for at least the next ~ week.  The ECMWF ensemble is dry through about Nov 12 with only a median 0.07" of water at Bridger over ten days. The GFS ensemble is similarly dry with only a hint of a pattern change beyond 7–10 days.

The early jump is the end of Sunday–Monday's storm.

Sadly, this is going to have me worried about faceting with the shallow snowpack near the ground. Clear, cold, long November nights mean we’ll get a strong temperature gradient between the ground and snowpack surface. This will be most prominent on shaded upper elevation north and east aspects, but we’ll just have to keep an eye on it and see how things shake out. I’m sure the Gallatin National Forest Avalanche Center (GNFAC) will be all over this, so make sure to keep an eye on any updates they put out especially once snow starts flying again.

Friday, October 30, 2026

Up Next

Me, patiently waiting for ski season:

After some great weather the past couple of days, it looks like another storm is on tap later this weekend.

Halloween (Saturday): Mild with breezy south winds; a few evening showers are possible. Hopefully it won’t be enough to negatively impact Trick-or-treaters.

Sunday: Showers develop in the afternoon. The storm will begin pretty warm with high rain/snow lines around 8,000 feet. By Sunday PM, however, a cold front will move through and snow levels should drop towards valley floors (though I'm not expecting any significant valley floor accumulations).

Below is a time-height cross section at Bozeman. It’s a little complicated but is a nice way to view storms moving through at a given location. Time goes from right to left. Times are in UTC ("Z"); subtract 7 hours for Mountain Time after Sunday's time change, so Mon 00Z is 5 PM Sunday. You can see on Sunday how southerly flow above 700 mb (middle part of the atmosphere) increases ahead of the storm/front with increasing moisture (darker greens). The blue line is the freezing level, so you can see that’s pretty high near 700–750mb initially (~8,000–10,000 ft). By Sunday PM, though, the blue line rapidly drops along with a switch in the wind direction (cold front passage) towards the NW and an increase in moisture throughout the column. You can see the blue line never reaches the surface, hence the higher snow levels and lack of significant accumulation for the valley.

Totals: 2-4 inches+ above mid-mountain at Bridger and Big Sky.

Below is another view of the storm system showing the potential range of outcomes from the 5th wettest to 5th driest ensemble members. You can think of each ensemble member as changing a very tiny parameter in the model to account for the fact that models are imperfect representations. The idea is that if you tweak enough of these slightly, you can capture most of the potential outcomes.

The bottom panels are the total water content at Bridger and Big Sky (rain/mix at the beginning, snow at the end), with the ensemble average in gray.

Beyond Monday, it is looking dry through mid-late week. Weather looks great for Tuesday, so nobody has an excuse for not getting out and voting on Election Day!
