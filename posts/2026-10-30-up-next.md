Me, patiently waiting for ski season:

After some great weather the past couple of days, it looks like another storm is on tap later this weekend. 

Halloween (Saturday): Mostly dry and mild with breezy south winds; however, a few stray evening showers are possible. Hopefully it won’t be enough to impact Trick-or-treaters.

Sunday: Showers develop in the afternoon. The storm will begin pretty warm with high rain/snow lines around 8000 feet. By Sunday PM, however, a cold front will move through and snow levels should drop towards valley floors (though am not expecting any significant valley floor accumulations).

Below is a Time Height Cross Section at Bozeman. It’s a little complicated but is a nice way to view storms moving through at a given location. Time goes from right-to-left. You can see on Sunday how southerly flow above 700 mb (middle part of atmosphere) increases ahead of the storm/front with increasing moisture (darker greens). The blue line is the freezing level, so you can that’s pretty high near 700–750mb initially (10,000 ft+). By Sunday PM, though, the blue line rapidly drops along with a switch in the wind direction (cold front passage) towards the NW and increase in moisture throughout the column. You can see the blue line never reaches the surface, hence the higher snow levels and lack of significant accumulation for the Valley.

![](/assets/images/GFSTH_KBZN2026103006F084.png)

Totals: 2-4 inches+ above mid-mountain at Bridger and Big Sky.

This is another view of the storm system showing the potential range of outcomes from the wettest to driest ensemble members. You can think of each ensemble as changing a very tiny parameter in the model to account for the fact that models are imperfect representations. The idea is that if you tweak enough of these slightly you can capture most of the potential outcomes.

Beyond Monday, it is looking dry through mid-late week. Weather looks great for Tuesday, so nobody has an excuse for not getting out and voting on Election Day!

-Trey
