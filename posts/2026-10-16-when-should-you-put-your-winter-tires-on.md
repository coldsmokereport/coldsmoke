After the early week storm, we're quickly thawing out and it looks like beautiful weather for this weekend. Hope you all are able to get out and enjoy it! The topic of today's post is something I've seen floating around the interwebs and common talk of the town around this time of year: When should I do my winter tire swap? I'm going to try to answer this with a bit of science and the latest weather forecasts.

We technically did already have our first snowfall that led to some road accumulations with the previous storm. However, road conditions only seemed dicey for maybe \~12-18 hours during the worst of the snow late Sun - early Mon. For those that held out and haven't swapped yet, you will be rewarded :). More on that in a bit. I should also add a couple of caveats here that may affect decision making. If you have to commute over a higher elevations like Bozeman Pass, for instance, that probably bumps up these timelines a bit. Also, a lot likely depends on the traction quality of your current tire set. If you already have decent All Season tires, that allows for more risk tolerance. Okay, let's get into the #s!

![](/coldsmoke/assets/images/01_tireswap_timing_oct16.png)

For the Oct 10–12 storm you can see that we were right at our climatological average for the first snowfall date, however, a bit ahead of schedule for the typical first snowfall accumulations > 1". Week-average highs dropping below 45ºF is used as a crude threshold here to weigh the potential for snowfall so more effectively stick to roadways; colder incipient roads/surfaces will collect snowfall more effectively. Typically early November is when those temperatures are consistently colder.

Another consideration is the number of "warm days" left (from both a climatological and forecast standpoint). Basically, with the warmer weather that's expected over the next 5–7 days, we're still expecting another 10+ days with highs over 60ºF this year. More than half of these are this next week. With this information, if you're able to hold out, waiting at least another week, maybe 2 would prevent you from driving on warmer roads (and potentially wearing down your tires a bit faster). If you're not driving a lot, it's probably no biggie, but if you are, it's worth factoring in, in addition to increased fuel costs if you swap to winter tires too early.

![](/coldsmoke/assets/images/04_warm_days_left.png)

Finally, looking at the current forecast, it's likely we'll be above average in terms of temperatures for the next \~week before we may see our next storm system late next week.
