I’ll keep this post short and sweet. After being stuck in the fog for much of the day in town (Bozeman), it was nice to finally see the sun peak out. Cold air/snow on the ground plus a quick ridge building led to a brief inversion across our area the past 24-48 hours. Looking at the Bridger temperatures peaking in the mid-upper 40s vs. 30s in town that inversion was quite apparent. 

A quick hitting storm will move in later this evening. Looking out to the west you can see the clouds associated with that approaching now.

![](/assets/images/storm.jpg "Storm clouds")

Currently a line of showers and thunderstorms to our W/S is moving quickly towards our area. This should begin affecting Big Sky and then Bozeman/Bridgers by mid-late evening. I’d expect graupel in the mountains associated with this initial convective activity (which may limit accumulation but does provide decent water content typically). Then maybe some lingering snow showers overnight behind the quick frontal passage. Overall, I’d expect a few inches of snow for the mountains. This time the flow is out of the WSW, so don’t expect a big skunk around Big Sky. Currently a winter weather advisory is in effect with the NWS calling for 2–5 inches.


-Trey
