The atmospheric river is living up to its name thus far! Overnight we had 1–2 inches of snowfall and a whopping 0.6 inches of SWE - true Sierra Cement. This is great news for base building and exactly the kind of snow we need this time of year. Models previously had this initial wave producing only \~0.1 inch of snowfall water equivalent (SWE), so this is great news. The winds have also been coming in strong as expected with 50mph+ gusts recorded overnight. Winds will likely remain gusty through the morning but should begin to easy a bit in the PM and especially by tomorrow. 

We should be in a bit of a lull this morning for precipitation, but that will quickly change by this PM. Temperatures will begin on the warmer side but will quickly drop later this afternoon. Nearly every forecast model and ensemble is showing at least 4 inches of snowfall for Bridger and Big Sky. There is a bit of variability in the exact positioning of the strongest band of precipitation, and Bridger is closer to the edge of the primary precipitation shield, which makes me a bit nervous. Regardless, I think the floor of the forecast is reasonably high confidence. The upper end is where things are a bit more uncertain (pending that exact band placement). Thus I think 4–8+ inches is a reasonable call for the mountains. In town, rain should change to snow this evening/overnight and we should end up with a couple of inches accumulation.
