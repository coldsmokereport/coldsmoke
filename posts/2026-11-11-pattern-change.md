The good news is the breakdown in the ridging, which has had a stranglehold on the northwest U.S., appears to finally be within reach! A little sooner than model guidance had indicated previously, a “ridge-busting” trough will coincide with a cold front moving through over the weekend. This will likely be a mostly dry storm, but it helps set us up for next week.

The second, wetter storm, the one initially highlighted in my previous post, was originally thought to come all in one piece. However, the models have been showing that offshore low now coming in a few waves, and there’s still a great deal of uncertainty in the timing and amounts.  The European model suite (ensemble and AI) has a slightly earlier start on Monday afternoon-evening, whereas the GEFS ensembles have the storm arriving on Tuesday (and GFS single run even later). The GEFS ensembles were previously showing as early as Monday AM in earlier runs over the weekend, hence why I think there’s still a large uncertainty and low confidence in this forecast. Below are some of the SWE (Snowfall water equivalent) numbers from the models. Generally about 0.1" of SWE equates to 1" of snow, however, there are many different factors. For example, _cold smoke_ often has very little water (0.05" or less for 1" of snow) and more air.
